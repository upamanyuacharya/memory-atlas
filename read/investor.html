The shortlist

The names that structurally define the map — chokepoint monopolies, scale players and pure-plays — and exactly why each one matters.

Chokepoint monopolies

Single points of failure — if any one stalls, the whole stack stalls. Highest barriers, near-impossible to displace.

  • TSMC TSM
    Makes the GPU AND the CoWoS packaging that bonds HBM to it — everything routes through its fabs. CoWoS booked to 2027.
  • ASML ASML
    A 100% EUV-lithography monopoly. No sub-7nm chip — logic or HBM base die — exists without an ASML machine.
  • Ajinomoto 2802.T
    ~95% of ABF film, the insulating layer in every high-end chip package. The quietest monopoly in the whole chain.

The scale players

The deepest revenue pools — where the AI build-out shows up first and largest in the P&L.

  • NVIDIA NVDA
    The demand engine. Sets the roadmap, buys the most HBM and optics, and pre-buys supply to lock out rivals.
  • SK Hynix 000660.KS
    The HBM bellwether — #1 in the scarcest component, first to qualify HBM4, ~60–70% of Rubin's allocation — and the biggest beneficiary of the DRAM price spike it is causing.
  • Broadcom AVGO
    Switching (Tomahawk) + custom XPUs for hyperscalers; the anchor of the merchant-silicon / UALink camp.
  • Marvell MRVL
    The most complete optical stack — ~70% DSP share + Celestial photonic fabric + custom AI silicon.

The pure-plays

Concentrated bets — smaller floats, high leverage to one specific part of the build-out.

  • Astera Labs ALAB
    The cleanest pure-play on AI connectivity — retimers, CXL, fabric switches. Wins the interconnect war either way.
  • Lumentum LITE
    The laser chokepoint — the only volume supplier of 200G/lane EML lasers, capacity locked through 2027; NVIDIA put $2B in (Mar 2026) to lock it.
  • BE Semiconductor BESI
    The hybrid-bonding tool the next HBM generations require. Tiny float, enormous optionality.

The roadmap — and the scorecard

What happens when, and who benefits. Entries whose date has passed carry a verdict: the atlas grades its own forecasts.

2025 · H2shipping
First production cloud CXL + photonic switches shipPartial

Azure ships CXL-attached memory (Astera Leo) — the first in a commercial cloud. NVIDIA Quantum-X Photonics and Broadcom's Tomahawk-6 Davisson (102.4 Tb/s CPO) begin sampling to tier-1 hyperscalers.

What actually happened — Leo CXL went live on Azure M-series on 18 Nov 2025 — as a preview, not GA. Davisson is shipping as the first 102.4T CPO Ethernet switch, but Quantum-X Photonics only went commercial in early 2026, a quarter or two late. Direction right, photonics timing slipped. (graded Sept 2026)

2026 · H1catalyst
HBM4 qualifies at NVIDIA (Rubin)Happened

SK Hynix and Samsung deliver HBM4 — the next bandwidth leap (a 2,048-bit bus). TSMC races CoWoS toward ~130k wafers/month. Whoever qualifies first wins the allocation.

What actually happened — All three suppliers qualified and in production, confirmed by Jensen Huang on 5 Jun 2026 — Samsung began mass production in February, SK Hynix qualified first and took ~60–70% of the allocation. CoWoS is tracking to ~130k wafers/month by year-end. (graded Sept 2026)

2026bottleneck
1.6T optics ramp & the EML-laser squeezeHappened

1.6T transceivers ramp (InnoLight volume, Marvell DSP inside). EML lasers sell out through 2027 after NVIDIA's ~$4B Lumentum/Coherent lockup. ABF substrate back in shortage (Ibiden). Scarcity = pricing power.

What actually happened — InnoLight's Q1 revenue rose 207% on 1.6T; every EML maker's capacity is under long-term agreements through 2027 after NVIDIA's $2B-each investments in Coherent and Lumentum (Mar 2026); ABF supply gap ~10% in H2 2026 with Ajinomoto pricing up to 30% higher. What we missed: conventional DRAM going into shortage too. (graded Sept 2026)

2026 · H2catalyst
Rubin samples, DRAM shortage bites, the rules get reviewedOpen

Rubin (288 GB HBM4, 22 TB/s) samples in Q4 with 16-Hi HBM4 stacks; AMD MI400 carries the first UALink silicon; Spectrum-X Photonics ships. DRAM contract prices keep rising as HBM takes ~22% of wafers. Two scheduled policy shocks: China's gallium/germanium reprieve to the US expires 27 Nov, and BIS reviews the HBM export rule (3A090.c) in December.

The interconnect war goes live — and the fabs arrive

Rubin in volume from Q1 on HBM4 (TSMC base dies). Intel Diamond Rapids finally lands, a year late; UALink products ship broadly; Astera's NVLink-Fusion hybrid racks and Scorpio X scale.
