In plain English

Picture bolting a stack of ultra-fast memory chips directly onto the GPU so data barely has to travel. That's HBM. It is the scarcest, most fought-over part in all of AI: only three companies can make it, it costs 5–10× normal memory, and it is sold out through 2027. If you remember one thing from this whole map, make it this.

What it is

High Bandwidth Memory — DRAM dies stacked 8–12 high with through-silicon vias, bonded to the GPU package via a silicon interposer. A single stack runs a 1,024-bit bus. The single most fought-over component in AI.

The business

The center of gravity. A 3-way oligopoly (SK Hynix, Samsung, Micron) makes the stacks — all three are now qualified on NVIDIA's Rubin HBM4, with SK Hynix holding ~60–70% of that allocation. TSMC CoWoS packaging is the real ceiling on supply (and now makes SK Hynix's base die too); Besi/ASML/Japan materials gate it from below. HBM is ~5–10× the cost/GB of DDR, is sold out through 2027, and is eating the DRAM wafers commodity memory used to get — see The DRAM Cycle.

Where the chokepoint comes from

Only three companies on earth can make HBM (SK Hynix, Samsung, Micron) — and even their output is capped from outside: every stack must be bonded to the GPU on TSMC's CoWoS packaging line (sold out into 2027) and its leading DRAM dies are printed on ASML's EUV machines (a literal monopoly). All three are sold out through 2027 and filling only 50–70% of what customers ask for, so allocation, not price, decides who ships GPUs.
