In plain English

Instead of plugging fibre-optic cables into the edge of a switch, you build the light engine right into the chip package — less power, far more bandwidth. Broadcom and NVIDIA are first out; startups Ayar and Lightmatter push it even closer to the GPU. Real technology, but mostly a 2028-and-beyond story at scale.

What it is

Move the optics into the switch/GPU package — millimetres of silicon instead of centimetres of lossy copper. Broadcom's Davisson (102.4 Tb/s) and NVIDIA's Spectrum-X are sampling; Ayar and Lightmatter push optics right beside the compute die.

The business

CPO helps Broadcom, NVIDIA, the laser makers, TSMC (Si-photonics foundry) and pure-plays Ayar/Lightmatter. It threatens pluggable-module volume (InnoLight, Fabrinet) — but only from ~2028. Marvell paid $3.25B for Celestial AI, the valuation anchor for the whole theme.

Bottleneck severity

High
Where the chokepoint comes from

Manufacturing yield, not demand: attaching fibres to silicon at scale is still unsolved, there's no interoperable standard yet, and every CPO design needs the same scarce lasers as everything else.
